"It actually works quite well." Really?

Many buoy fields have been managed the same way for years.

The phone rings. A place is reserved. Someone collects payment in the evening. You know your guests.

And yes, it works.

But the key question is not whether it works.

It is whether it makes economic sense.

Because an analog buoy field often costs more than operators think. Not only money, but also time, nerves, and growth potential.

Let us look at it honestly.

## 1. Hidden vacancies

One often underestimated point is empty buoys.

Example:

- 20 buoys
- average price per night: 35 €
- season: 90 high-season days

If only 2 buoys per night remain empty on average because nobody knows they are available, that means:

**2 buoys x 35 € x 90 days = 6,300 € lost revenue per season**

And that is a conservative calculation.

Vacancies often do not happen because there is no demand. They happen because there is no visibility.

## 2. Time is money - even in family businesses

How many calls do you receive per day during the season?

Let us assume:

- 10 calls per day
- 5 minutes per call

That is almost 50 minutes per day.

Over 90 days, that adds up to **75 hours of phone time**.

And that is only reservations. It does not include:

- follow-up questions
- cancellations
- price discussions
- chasing unpaid stays

Time you do not spend in the restaurant, on maintenance, or with your family.

Digital bookings reduce this workload massively.

## 3. Cash payment and uncertainty

Collecting cash sounds simple.

But it means:

- needing to be on site
- trips with the dinghy
- dependence on time of day and weather
- missing documentation

Then there is the uncertainty around unreported overnight stays or last-minute departures.

A structured system creates clear allocation, transparent payment, and traceable receipts.

That creates not only order, but also trust.

## 4. No data, no planning

Without a digital overview, you often only know roughly: "This season was quite good."

But:

- Which weeks were strongest?
- Which buoys are especially popular?
- What is your real average occupancy?

Without data, too much remains based on feeling.

With numbers, you can:

- adjust prices
- define premium places
- plan investments
- check funding opportunities

Entrepreneurial decisions need clarity.

## 5. Lack of transparency slows growth

Many operators could get more out of their buoy field.

For example:

- cooperation with restaurants
- premium front-row buoys
- season extension
- dynamic pricing

But without a system, everything remains manual and time-consuming.

Digitally managed buoy fields create new opportunities without creating more stress.

More revenue should not mean more work.

## 6. Let us calculate realistically

A small example for a 25-buoy field:

- average price: 35 €
- average analog occupancy: 70 %
- season: 100 days

**25 x 35 € x 100 x 0.70 = 61,250 € revenue**

If better visibility and plannable booking increase occupancy to 85 %:

**25 x 35 € x 100 x 0.85 = 74,375 €**

**Difference: 13,125 € per season.**

And that is without adding a single buoy.

Only through better organization.

## 7. The biggest cost factor: Constant stress

What no spreadsheet shows:

- constant availability
- discussions in the bay
- uncertainty around occupancy
- dependence on personal presence

A system that simplifies booking, overview, and documentation reduces exactly this pressure.

And over the long term, that is priceless.

## It is not about digitalization. It is about profitability.

An analog buoy field works. But it limits.

It limits occupancy.

It limits transparency.

It limits growth.

A digital solution does not mean losing control. It means gaining overview.

And overview is the foundation for entrepreneurial freedom.

If your buoy field is your business, it should also be managed like a business.
