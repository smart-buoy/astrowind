"Digital? That is not for me."

Many buoy fields have been run successfully for decades.

With experience. With trust. With personal contact.

That is exactly why caution around digital solutions is understandable.

But often it is not facts that slow operators down. It is assumptions.

Here are five common myths and what is really behind them.

## Myth 1: "This is far too complicated."

**The concern:** New technology means stress, training, and more room for mistakes.

**The reality:** Good digital buoy management has to be easier than your current system. Otherwise, it adds no value.

What matters:

- a clear interface
- all buoys visible at a glance
- simple booking overview
- personal support when questions come up

If a system takes work off your hands instead of creating more, it is not extra effort. It is relief.

## Myth 2: "I will lose control over my buoy field."

Many operators worry that digitalization will make them less flexible.

In most cases, the opposite is true.

With a digital overview, you can see:

- who booked and when
- which buoy is free
- how high your occupancy is
- which revenue is already secured

Control comes from transparency.

And transparency creates entrepreneurial freedom.

You still decide your prices, availability, and rules. You simply do it with a better overview.

## Myth 3: "My guests do not want this."

It is a common thought.

But reality on the water is changing. Many skippers are used to booking accommodation, restaurants, or flights digitally.

What they value:

- clarity
- real-time availability
- plannability
- digital confirmation

Digital booking does not replace personal contact. It complements it.

You can still speak with your guests. Just without the pressure of coordinating every reservation by phone.

## Myth 4: "This will only create tax problems."

This concern often comes from uncertainty.

In practice, structured and documented payment processing creates more traceability.

Digital booking means:

- clear receipts
- transparent revenue overview
- traceable transactions

Instead of disorder, you get structure. And structure is almost always safer than improvisation.

Of course, every model should fit local legal requirements. But digitalization itself is not the risk. Lack of overview is more likely to be one.

## Myth 5: "It is not worth it for my small field."

Smaller buoy fields in particular can benefit strongly from better visibility and higher occupancy.

One example: if you generate just one additional booking per day, that can make a difference of several thousand euros over a season.

Then there is the time saved.

Fewer calls. Less coordination. Less uncertainty.

Digitalization is not a question of size. It is a question of efficiency.

## What really matters: Your day-to-day work

In the end, this is not about technology.

It is about whether you:

- have less stress in the evening
- understand your occupancy better
- need fewer difficult discussions
- have a clearer view of your revenue

If a system helps with exactly that, it is not a risk. It is a step forward.

The maritime world can become modern without losing its values.

## Growth starts in the mind

Most barriers are not technical. They are mental.

Digital buoy management does not mean giving up tradition.

It means making it ready for the future.

More overview.

More plannability.

More financial stability.

And in the end, more peace of mind for you as an operator.
